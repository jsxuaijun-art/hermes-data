# Hong Kong Offshore Claim Guide

## 1. Purpose
This guide supports fact-first analysis of Hong Kong profits tax territorial source / offshore claim issues.

## 2. Core principle
Do not conclude that profit is offshore merely because:
- customers are overseas;
- goods never enter Hong Kong;
- contracts are denominated in foreign currency;
- the company has an overseas bank account.

Identify the operations that actually produced the profits and where those operations were carried out.

## 3. Trading profits
Review at least:
- supplier sourcing;
- customer sourcing;
- negotiation;
- pricing;
- contract conclusion;
- order acceptance;
- procurement;
- sales execution;
- inventory / shipping route;
- staff location;
- management and risk control;
- related-party involvement.

## 4. Service income
Review:
- who performs the service;
- where the personnel are located;
- where the key activities are performed;
- where contracts are negotiated and concluded;
- where management and control occur;
- where the customer receives / uses the service.

## 5. Evidence
Typical evidence:
- purchase / sales contracts;
- PO / SO;
- emails and negotiation records;
- travel records;
- shipping and logistics documents;
- invoices;
- bank records;
- staff records;
- organization charts;
- board / management records;
- supplier / customer correspondence.

## 6. IRD enquiry readiness
An offshore position must be supportable by contemporaneous evidence. Mark unsupported positions as `Needs Evidence`.

## 7. FSIE boundary
Do not treat FSIE and ordinary trading-profit source analysis as the same question.
If foreign-sourced passive income may fall within FSIE, route to the FSIE guide separately.

## 8. Output
- Facts
- Key profit-producing operations
- Hong Kong activities
- Offshore activities
- Evidence available
- Evidence missing
- Preliminary source assessment
- Risk level
- IRD enquiry readiness
- Next actions
